# Maple Intel Brand Foundation

## Status

Working strategic direction. This document should be agreed before visual design or implementation begins.

## Brand proposition

Maple Intel develops intelligent reporting for NHS operations. Its work covers
operational analytics, reporting and the supporting information structures.

It combines technical delivery with first-hand operational understanding: the work is designed not only to report activity, but to help people understand demand, flow, performance, capacity and outcomes.

## Primary positioning statement

> Intelligent reporting for NHS operations.

Supporting explanation:

> Maple Intel develops reporting focused on demand, flow, capacity and
> performance, beginning with how the service operates and how the information
> will be used.

The proposition is deliberately specific and should not be diluted with a list
of adjacent sectors or replaced by generic language about “unlocking data” or
“transforming insights”.

## Market focus

### Primary specialism

- ambulance-service operations;
- urgent and emergency healthcare operations;
- other NHS services with complex demand, flow, workforce, resource or performance data.

The public website should maintain this NHS focus. Examples from contact-centre
environments are included only where they demonstrate reporting relevant to NHS
operations.

## Audience

### Primary audience: potential clients

They need to establish quickly:

- whether Maple Intel understands their type of problem;
- whether the practice can work with imperfect operational data;
- whether it can design a reliable path from source data to decision;
- what credible evidence supports those claims;
- how to begin a conversation.

Likely client readers include operational leaders, performance and information leads, analytics managers, programme leads and service owners who need to understand or improve operational performance.

### Secondary audience: recruiters and engagement intermediaries

They need to establish quickly:

- whether Maple Intel has the relevant technical capabilities;
- whether the work is real and sufficiently complex;
- whether Stephen understands operational requirements as well as tools;
- whether Maple Intel can undertake a defined contract, interim or embedded assignment;
- how to discuss a specific opportunity.

This audience should be served by the same evidence as clients. It should not determine the brand, navigation or primary calls to action.

### Technical peers and assessors

They may inspect:

- data modelling and transformation decisions;
- SQL, Power Query, DAX and semantic-model design;
- validation methods;
- treatment of time, state, workflow and data quality;
- the reasoning behind report design.

## Desired perception

Maple Intel should feel:

- technically rigorous;
- operationally informed;
- calm and precise;
- candid about complexity;
- dependable;
- specialist rather than generalist;
- confident without being promotional.

It should resemble a small, expert analytics practice—not a freelance template and not a miniature imitation of a large consultancy.

## Differentiation

The distinction is not simply proficiency with Power BI. Maple Intel brings together:

1. operational understanding;
2. analytics engineering;
3. semantic and dimensional modelling;
4. decision-focused report design;
5. validation and governance.

The website should repeatedly demonstrate the relationship between these disciplines. Technical tools are supporting evidence, not the whole proposition.

## Brand principles

### Evidence before assertion

Prefer a case study, model, screenshot or explained decision to an unsupported claim.

### Operational questions before visualisations

Begin with the decision, workflow or performance question. Show how the data and report were designed around it.

### Clarity without oversimplification

Make complex work understandable while retaining enough technical depth to be credible.

### Restraint signals confidence

Avoid exaggerated claims, decorative metrics and fashionable consultancy language. Let the quality of the work carry the message.

### Confidentiality is part of professionalism

Anonymisation should be explicit where relevant. Case studies must demonstrate the method without exposing sensitive organisations, people or operational details.

## Voice

Use:

- direct, concrete sentences;
- first person when describing Stephen’s actions;
- precise technical terms where they add meaning;
- active verbs such as designed, modelled, reconstructed, validated and delivered;
- measured descriptions of outcomes.

Avoid:

- “data-driven solutions”;
- “unlock the power of your data”;
- “actionable insights” without explaining the action;
- “cutting-edge”, “world-class” or “revolutionary”;
- claims that cannot be evidenced;
- long tool lists presented as a substitute for capability.

## Naming convention

- **Maple Intel** is the practice and primary website identity.
- **Stephen Clinton** is the named practitioner behind the work.
- Page copy should make the relationship clear early rather than presenting Maple Intel as a larger team.

## Working message hierarchy

1. **What:** Power BI development and analytics engineering.
2. **Where:** Primarily ambulance services and other operational NHS environments.
3. **Why credible:** Real case studies showing source-to-decision work.
4. **What is distinctive:** Technical delivery informed by senior operational experience.
5. **Next step:** Discuss a project, assignment or analytics problem.

## Engagement language

Present Maple Intel as an independent consultancy that can be engaged for:

- defined analytics projects;
- consultancy and advisory work;
- contract assignments;
- interim or embedded delivery roles.

Prefer “Discuss a project”, “Discuss an analytics requirement”, “Engage Maple Intel” or “Contact Stephen” to “Hire me”. The latter makes the site resemble a job-seeking portfolio rather than a business.
