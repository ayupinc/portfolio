Telephony Demand & Agent Activity

What I Did

  • Designed the Power BI semantic model supporting demand, telephony and agent activity reporting
  • Built Power Query transformations to standardise reporting categories, activity groupings and operational classifications
  • Developed DAX measures covering demand, service performance, utilisation, activity analysis and percentile reporting
  • Created five Power BI reports comprising more than forty reporting pages
  • Designed drill-through and investigation workflows supporting operational review and individual activity analysis
  • Produced KPI definitions, user guidance and reporting documentation
  • Optimised report performance across several large datasets and reporting workloads

Context

I developed this reporting solution to provide operational visibility of demand, workforce utilisation and agent activity across a high-volume contact centre environment handling approximately 2 million inbound calls and 500,000 outbound calls annually.

Existing reporting solutions provided either high-level interval statistics or call-level outputs that became unreliable following changes to the telephony environment. Neither approach provided a consistent view of demand, individual call activity and workforce utilisation within a single reporting framework.

Although I had already developed the underlying analytical model, covered separately within the Analytics Engineering section, the reporting challenge was different. My objective was to create a Power BI reporting platform capable of supporting operational management, workforce planning, performance analysis and individual activity review from a common semantic model.

Key Reporting Challenges

  • Combining demand, call activity and workforce utilisation within a single reporting model
  • Supporting both organisation-level reporting and individual activity investigation
  • Converting telephony events into operational concepts meaningful to users
  • Defining utilisation measures from multiple overlapping activity states
  • Managing large volumes of call and activity data without compromising report performance
  • Providing consistent KPI definitions across multiple reports and user groups

Data Model

The reporting model was built around four primary fact tables representing calls, agent call activity, agent sessions and agent activity periods.

A key objective was enabling users to move seamlessly between organisational demand, individual calls, agent sessions and detailed activity records without requiring separate reporting models.

I designed the semantic model around operational concepts rather than source-system structures. Calls, sessions and activities were linked through shared dimensions and supporting lookup tables, creating a reporting framework capable of supporting both summary analysis and detailed investigation.

Power Query transformations were used extensively to standardise queue structures, classify call types, create reporting hierarchies and generate operationally meaningful groupings from source-system attributes. Time-band analysis, queue categorisation and activity classifications were implemented within the reporting layer to improve usability and consistency.

The resulting semantic model supported demand analysis, utilisation reporting, KPI monitoring and session-level investigation from a single reporting framework.

KPI Design and Operational Interpretation

One of the more significant reporting challenges was defining workforce utilisation in a way that reflected operational reality.

Although the telephony platform recorded large volumes of activity information, many of the native metrics were difficult for operational users to interpret consistently. Different activity states overlapped, some metrics were recorded differently depending on context and several measures required additional interpretation before they could support operational reporting.

To address this, I developed a reporting framework based around a hierarchy of operational activity concepts including session time, assignment time, contact time, active time, waiting time and not-ready time. These concepts provided users with a clearer understanding of how logged-in time was being utilised and allowed performance discussions to focus on operational behaviour rather than telephony terminology.

Additional measures supported percentile analysis, duration analysis, service performance monitoring and investigation of activity patterns across teams and reporting periods.

By documenting both the calculations and their operational interpretation, I established a consistent reporting language across multiple reports and user groups.

Report Design

I designed the reporting platform around three primary themes:

  • Demand and workload analysis
  • Service performance monitoring
  • Agent activity and utilisation

Pages focused on:

  • Demand by queue, team and reporting period
  • Answer and abandonment performance
  • Handling time analysis
  • Workforce utilisation
  • Agent activity patterns
  • Session-level investigation
  • Operational KPI monitoring

Interactive filtering, drill-through and contextual navigation enabled users to move from high-level demand reporting to individual calls, sessions and activity journals.

My design objective was to support both routine operational monitoring and detailed investigation without requiring users to leave the reporting environment.

Additional guidance pages, KPI definitions and navigation support were incorporated to improve report usability and ensure consistent interpretation of metrics across different user groups.

Outcome and Usage

The completed solution established a unified reporting platform for demand, service performance and workforce activity.

Operational teams were able to analyse demand patterns, monitor service performance, investigate individual activity and understand workforce utilisation using a common set of governed metrics and definitions.

The reporting framework removed reliance on externally generated reporting outputs and provided a sustainable platform for future reporting development.

By combining semantic modelling, KPI governance and operational reporting design, I created a reporting environment capable of supporting both day-to-day operational management and detailed activity investigation across millions of annual telephony interactions.
